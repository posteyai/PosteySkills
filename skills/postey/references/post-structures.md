# Post Structures

Part of the `postey` skill. Loaded on demand; see SKILL.md "Content Flows".

Eighteen structures, each with the condition that selects it, the shape it takes, its length
rule, and the way it usually fails. Derived from measured top-vs-bottom quintile analysis of
high-performing operator accounts on X.

**Pick the structure from the material, not from preference.** Every entry starts with WHEN
because that is the decision. If no WHEN matches what the user actually has, the post is not
ready — go back for material rather than forcing a shape onto thin content.

Pair this with [hook-formulas.md](hook-formulas.md) for the opening line and the AI-tells scrub,
and [platform-archetypes.md](platform-archetypes.md) for how the chosen structure is dressed per
network.

## The density rule

Two lengths perform. Under ~140 characters, where every word is load-bearing. Or over ~600,
where genuinely new facts appear on every line.

**Between 200 and 500 characters is a dead zone** — long enough to look like effort, short
enough to carry nothing new. A draft that lands there is either a sentence padded out or a real
post cut short. Fix it by compressing to one line or by adding actual material. Never by
trimming to "about the right length".

Length is not the variable. Density is.

## 1. The Aphorism

**WHEN** the material reduces to one true sentence that most people would dispute, and you have
no new facts to add.
**SHAPE** One sentence. Symmetry, reversal, or a both-halves-true structure. No explanation, no
follow-up line.
**LENGTH** Under 20 words. In the sampled corpus, top aphorism posts averaged ~84 characters
against ~168 for the same account's bottom quintile.
**FAILURE MODE** Adding a second sentence that explains the first. It roughly halves
performance. If it needs explaining, you want structure 3.

## 2. The Label Stack

**WHEN** you have one piece of advice and three to six concrete instances of it.
**SHAPE** `[Label]: [claim].` then rapid-fire fragments, each a real instance, then a closer that
names the underlying principle.
**LENGTH** ~250 characters as ONE dense block. Winners averaged 1.7 lines against 3.5 for
losers.
**FAILURE MODE** Breaking each fragment onto its own line. The density is the point; a vertical
list reads as filler.

## 3. The Mechanism Explainer

**WHEN** you have a claim about behaviour and can explain *why* the mind or the market produces
it.
**SHAPE** Claim → the mechanism → the instruction that follows from the mechanism.
**LENGTH** 200–400 characters, ONE paragraph. Winners averaged 1.2 lines; losers ran 9.2 lines
and 844 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Explaining the mechanism at essay length. The instruction is what gets saved.

## 4. The Gap Post

**WHEN** you can name two to four facts that are individually known but collectively imply
something nobody has said.
**SHAPE** Fact fragments, often a bare list → the tension between them → a closing line handing
the reader the opportunity or the unanswered question.
**LENGTH** 300–450 characters, three or four short paragraphs.
**FAILURE MODE** Answering your own question in the last line. Leave the gap open.

## 5. The Operator Confession

**WHEN** something in your own business is risky, went wrong, or contradicts what you sell.
**SHAPE** Name the risk or the miss in the first clause, with no cushion. Then the specifics.
Then what you are doing about it, or the trade-off you accept.
**LENGTH** 150–500 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Burying the admission below line one, or resolving it too neatly. A confession
that ends in a win is an ad.

## 6. The Sourced Observation

**WHEN** you talked to someone, ate with someone, or asked someone who actually knows.
**SHAPE** The provenance in the first clause → what they said, compressed → what it changed for
you.
**LENGTH** 200–450 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Name-dropping without a transferable insight. The reader should be able to use
it without knowing the person.

## 7. The Build Log

**WHEN** you are mid-problem and thinking out loud, with real numbers.
**SHAPE** First person, chronological, unpolished. State the numbers. Say which one is weak and
why. Include the thing you do not know yet. Pre-empt the obvious criticism.
**LENGTH** 800–1,500 characters. Winners averaged ~1,246 characters and 10.8 lines against ~222
and 2.7 for losers — a 5.6x length advantage.
**FAILURE MODE** Polishing it. The unresolved part is what draws replies.

## 8. The Receipts List

**WHEN** you spent money, time or effort learning something the reader would otherwise learn
expensively.
**SHAPE** A framing line naming the cost → bullets, each with a **reason attached**, written as
if texting a friend.
**LENGTH** No ceiling. The highest-bookmark structure in the corpus.
**FAILURE MODE** Bullets without reasons. "Hire slowly" is a platitude; "hire slowly because the
first bad hire sets the bar for the next five" is a receipt.

## 9. The Timeline

**WHEN** a chapter is ending — a launch, an exit, a role change, a year in review.
**SHAPE** Year or phase blocks with bullets under each. **Mix the enormous with the human** in
the same list. End on one word.
**LENGTH** Long, no ceiling.
**FAILURE MODE** All milestones, no texture. The small human entries are what make it readable.

## 10. The Contrast Open

**WHEN** two actors are responding to the same problem in opposite ways.
**SHAPE** "While A is doing X, B is doing Y" → the mechanism, explained plainly → a closing line
locating what is actually new, which is usually a decision rather than a technology.
**LENGTH** 600–1,000 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Picking a side in line one. The contrast does the work; the verdict comes last
or not at all.

## 11. The Reframe Hook

**WHEN** everyone is reading an event one way and you think it is about something else.
**SHAPE** "X didn't do A. It did B." in line one → a line naming what coverage is missing → the
evidence, with names, quotes, filings → the real point.
**LENGTH** 400–3,000 characters. Earn the length with facts on every line.
**FAILURE MODE** Contrarianism without evidence. If the reframe rests on opinion, it reads as a
take, and takes do not get bookmarked.

## 12. The Demo Drop

**WHEN** a tool did something that would have been a company two years ago.
**SHAPE** One line of framing → **the actual prompt or artifact, verbatim** → a flat,
understated verdict.
**LENGTH** Short frame, long artifact, three-word close.
**FAILURE MODE** Describing the output instead of showing it. The artifact is the post.

## 13. The Ship Note

**WHEN** you shipped something.
**SHAPE** What it does now. Who benefits. Stop.
**LENGTH** Under 25 words. No thread, no "excited to announce", no rocket emoji.
**FAILURE MODE** Turning it into a launch announcement. The restraint is the signal.

## 14. The Two-Types Split

**WHEN** the same situation produces opposite outcomes depending on one variable.
**SHAPE** "Two kinds of people [do X]:" → the losing behaviour → the winning behaviour → a rule
in two mirrored lines.
**LENGTH** 200–350 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Making one type a strawman. Both must be recognisable, or the reader is not in
the post.

## 15. The Number Compression

**WHEN** a price, cost or capability moved further than people realise.
**SHAPE** The number in line one → the source, named → a line relocating it into its real
category → historical analogies that make the scale land.
**LENGTH** 600–900 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** An unsourced number. One unverifiable figure discredits the rest.

## 16. The Human Post

**WHEN** an anniversary, a team moment, a person you admire, a loss.
**SHAPE** The occasion in one line → **specific small details about named real people** → what
that says about the thing that lasts. Warmth is allowed here and nowhere else.
**LENGTH** 300–700 characters.
**FAILURE MODE** Generic gratitude. Without specific detail it reads as performance.

## 17. The Zero Stack

**WHEN** the reader believes they are too far behind to start.
**SHAPE** A vertical stack of zeros or nulls → one line naming the only real difference.
**LENGTH** Under 40 words.
**FAILURE MODE** Explaining the stack. The visual carries it.

## 18. The Genuine Question

**WHEN** you actually want the answer and will act on it.
**SHAPE** One question. One line of why you are asking. Nothing else.
**LENGTH** Under 25 words.
**FAILURE MODE** Engagement bait. A question you already know the answer to reads as one, and
replies punish it.

## Porting a structure off X

> **Untested — this section is reasoning, not measurement.** Everything above comes from
> top-vs-bottom quintile analysis of real posts. Nothing below does: the corpus is X-only, so
> no data here says how these structures behave on LinkedIn, Instagram or TikTok. Treat it as a
> starting hypothesis, and say so if a user asks why a structure was chosen for a non-X platform.
> When the account has its own cross-platform history, `postey-analytics` on that account beats
> this table — use it instead.

One thing here **is** grounded: on Instagram and TikTok the structure governs the **spoken** hook,
not the caption. The caption follows its platform archetype
([platform-archetypes.md](platform-archetypes.md)), and the structure only shapes the first line.

The rest is inference from what each structure depends on:

| Structure depends on | So it likely needs | Structures |
|---|---|---|
| Value-per-line alone | nothing platform-specific | Receipts List, Two-Types Split, Mechanism Explainer |
| Room to develop a thought | a long-form surface | Operator Confession, Timeline, Human Post, Build Log |
| Compression to a single line | works as a spoken hook | Aphorism, Zero Stack, Two-Types Split |
| Reply culture, or pasting a raw artifact | a conversational text feed | Ship Note, Genuine Question, Demo Drop |
| A reader who follows a dense argument | X or LinkedIn behaviour | Reframe Hook, Number Compression |

Read the middle column, not the platform names — the dependency is the durable part, and it is
what tells you whether a structure survives a surface this table never anticipated.

## Self-check before returning a draft

1. Which structure is this, and does it obey that structure's length rule?
2. Is there a tension, surprise, or reversal? Name it in one clause. If you cannot, it is not
   ready.
3. Does every line make a claim the line above did not?
4. Density: is it under ~140 characters, or over ~600 with new facts throughout? If it is in the
   200–500 dead zone, cut it to a line or add material.
5. What makes someone reply? What makes someone bookmark? Answer both.
6. Run the AI-tells scrub in [hook-formulas.md](hook-formulas.md) — em-dashes, stacked
   period-rhythm, buzzwords, mission-statement closers.
7. Is every number and name verified?
8. Is there one detail only this author could have written?
9. Does the last line land on the non-obvious part?
10. Is any external link moved to the first reply? See [x-algorithm.md](x-algorithm.md).
